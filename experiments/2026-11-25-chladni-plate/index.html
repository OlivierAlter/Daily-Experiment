Chladni Plate

Sand on a steel plate, driven by one pure tone. At most pitches it just shivers. At the plate's own resonances it runs to the lines that stay still, and a figure appears.

What you are seeing. Ernst Chladni drew a violin bow along plates like this in 1787. Each figure is a standing wave: the sand is thrown off the parts that move and collects where the plate is still. Higher pitches give finer figures, because the pitch of a plate rises with the square of the number of lines.
